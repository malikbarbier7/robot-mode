# Keyword analysis — October 2026

**How this was done.** No search-volume tool was available: the InhouseSEO
trial has ended. Demand was read from Google's autocomplete (what people
actually type, ranked by popularity, US English) and competition from the
current top results. So this says *which phrasings exist* and *who holds them*,
not exact monthly volumes. Re-check with a keyword tool (Ahrefs, Semrush,
Google Keyword Planner) once the site is live, and with Search Console data
after a few weeks.

## Who ranks

- **Head terms** ("weekly schedule maker", "schedule maker", "weekly schedule
  template", "study schedule generator", "time blocking template"): Canva,
  Adobe Express, Visme, Lucid, Smartsheet, ClickUp, 101 Planners. Very strong
  domains with design-template pages. **Not winnable soon** for a new site.
- **AI angle**: many "generator" results now push AI (Taskade, Fotor, Scholarly,
  Kuse, MyMap). "No AI, deterministic, private" is a real differentiator, but
  searched rarely: use it in copy, not as a target.
- **Long-tail and advice** ("deep work schedule example", "how to plan your week
  effectively", "time blocking examples for students", "ideal weekly schedule
  template"): individual blogs, universities (Oregon State), Medium, Zapier,
  Shortform, Full Focus (Michael Hyatt's "Ideal Week"). **Winnable** with
  better, more concrete pages, especially ones that show a real week.

## Strategy

1. **The landing page** goes after the tool intent: "automatic weekly schedule
   maker", "free", "printable", "with times". Head terms will take long; the
   modifiers are where a new site can appear first.
2. ~~Template pages per audience~~ — built, then **dropped** (3 October 2026):
   a fixed week per audience doesn't fit people whose schedule changes (a
   student's timetable moves every term), which is the opposite of what
   Robot Mode is for. The template-style searches are left to the landing
   page and the guides.
3. **Three cornerstone articles** for planning advice, each leading into the
   machine:
   - **How to plan your week (and stop deciding every day)** → how to plan your
     week · effectively · as a student · to be productive · how to stick to a
     schedule.
   - **Time blocking: real examples, and a week that blocks itself** → time
     blocking examples · examples for students · time blocking template ·
     deep work schedule example.
   - **How to design your ideal week** → ideal weekly schedule · ideal week
     template · weekly schedule example · weekly schedule ideas. Replaces the
     planned "decision fatigue" article: those searches are definitions,
     owned by psychology sites, with little intent. Decision fatigue becomes a
     section of this article instead, where it explains why an ideal week works.
4. **Later**: ADHD-friendly planning ("how to plan your week with adhd", "time
   blocking for adhd", "how to stick to a schedule with adhd") is a large,
   underserved cluster that fits a fixed, printed week well. Worth an article
   once the first three are out, written carefully.
